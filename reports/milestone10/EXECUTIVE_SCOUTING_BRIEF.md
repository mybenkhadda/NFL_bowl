# Executive Scouting Brief — Combine Intelligence Lab

**For:** general managers, scouting directors, personnel analysts · **Length:** two pages · *Rendered 2026-10-14T14:25:56Z; numbers from the executed Milestone 09 mart and the 08B.6 evidence registry.*

## The bottom line

1. **The stopwatch hides real differences.** Of 3,266 pairs of same-position prospects whose forties are within 0.03 s, 2,097 differ on at least one tracked sprint measure by more than measurement noise.
2. **Some of those differences are new information; some are not.** *How quickly speed builds* (4 → 8 yd/s) repeats from attempt to attempt and is only partly explained by the official times. *Top speed* repeats too, but mostly restates the forty. The Lab tells you which is which, on every screen.
3. **None of it is a performance forecast.** Two independent tests found no improvement in predicting NFL movement or receiver separation over the official Combine numbers. Use the Lab to decide **what to look at next**, never to reorder a board.

## What it looks like on one decision

**Anthony Bradford vs Mason McCormick (OL) — both 5.08 s.**

| | Bradford | McCormick |
|---|---|---|
| Official forty | 5.08 s | 5.08 s |
| 10-yard split | 1.74 s | 1.71 s |
| Speed already carried at 10 yd | 7.59 yd/s | 9.10 yd/s |
| Speed at 20 yd | 9.11 yd/s | 8.77 yd/s |
| Time to build from 4 to 8 yd/s | 1.59 s | 0.68 s |

All five differences exceed measurement uncertainty. McCormick is far quicker through the first steps; Bradford carries more speed by 20 yards. McCormick's recording is in a lower evidence tier — the Lab says so beside the number.

**What the Lab tells you to do:** compare both on film on the first steps of a pull or a set and on pulls and climbs to the second level; if it matters to your scheme, time a 0–5 / 0–10 yd start with split timing.
**What it tells you not to conclude:** which of them will be the more effective NFL lineman.

## What the forty cannot see

NFL tracking (416,507 movement episodes) shows that games ask for much more than a straight-line sprint: braking (28 episodes per 100 eligible player-plays across positions), speed holding (11.8), repeated speed changes (8.2), re-acceleration after braking (7.4), redirection (5.9). **None of these has a physical analogue in the forty.**

**Decision implication:** for those demands, film is currently the only evidence. A short tracked deceleration or re-acceleration test is a candidate measurement — proposed, not proven.

## What you can and cannot infer

| You can say | You cannot say |
|---|---|
| "These two ran the same time with measurably different speed curves." | "The quicker starter will be the better player." |
| "This difference is larger than the athlete's own attempt-to-attempt variation." | "This measure predicts NFL production." |
| "This phase is physically closest to these observed game movements." | "He will meet that demand in pads, in traffic, in the fourth quarter." |
| "The forty does not measure braking or redirection; film or a new test must." | "A new test will improve our draft." |

## Why trust it

- Every number traces to an executed, versioned table; nothing is imputed — a missing measurement is shown as missing.
- Comparisons use one pre-declared matching rule (same position, forty within 0.03 s); case studies were chosen without looking at NFL outcomes.
- The tool abstains where evidence is thin, and its wording is machine-checked: no grades, rankings, probabilities or "hidden stars".
